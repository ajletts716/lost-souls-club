The pilgrimage

James and Red are twenty, from the suburbs, and driving south. The errand is small. Red needs new license plates from his father in Texas. Neither of them believes that is why they left.

In New Orleans they take a back room in a shotgun house in the 9th Ward. In an alley in the French Quarter they meet Jay and Puke, street kids who cross the country by freight train, and Jay's dog, Maggie. The boys think they have found true pilgrims, living in holy poverty like the old orders of ascetics.

For three nights the four of them drink, beg, and walk the Quarter. James is given a new name. Red feels his hold on James slipping. In the end, what the boys are searching for is not what the street kids are living.

ReadThe pilgrims

James

Pilgrim one. Suburban, 20 or 21.

Thoughtful, lost in his own life, and looking for answers. We see mostly through his eyes. He wants to belong and he wants the truth. He may not get both.

Red

Pilgrim two. Suburban, 20 or 21.

Charismatic and sure of himself. He has never had trouble getting what he wants, and he is desperate for something more. The road trip was his idea.

Puke

Street kid, 19 or 20. North Carolina.

Left home at sixteen. She has been to all 49 states on the mainland by freight train. Her dream is to reach Hawaii by cargo ship.

Jay

Street kid, 21 or 22. Virginia.

Raised in the church. Resentful and unpredictable. He travels with his dog, Maggie, and would rather be left in the shadows.

From the outline

1

Gas station

James wanders the aisles of a gas station. He approaches the counter. A girl works the register. He comments on her necklace. A cross with a wolf coming out of the top. The conversation is awkward but sweet.

7

The house, 9th Ward

In the house there is a pet pig in a dog bed. The man introduces the pig as Zeus. He leads the boys through the shotgun rooms into a back room, decorated to resemble a circus tent. They are never to enter the rest of the house. And they must always keep the back door locked.

14

The French Quarter

Jay and Puke are street kids, they say. They prefer this term. They left home and they travel by freight train. Jay pulls up his sleeve to reveal a tattoo. Squatter's rights, he says. Abandoned buildings, foreclosed homes. Other street kids leave signs.

32

Dark streets

The crew walk back to the house. Jay thanks James for the invitation. Then he asks him, How do you know we won't kill you in your sleep? James doesn't know how to answer. The same way you don't know we won't. The two grin at each other.
